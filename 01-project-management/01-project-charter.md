
# Enterprise AI Governance Readiness Workbench

## Project Charter — Version 1.0

**Project type:** Enterprise AI implementation portfolio  
**Industry scenario:** European financial services  
**Project duration:** 8-week simulated delivery baseline  
**Portfolio role:** AI Implementation Project Manager  
**Project status:** Initiation  
**Environment:** Demonstration / non-production  
**Company:** Fictional European Financial Services Group  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

A fictional European financial-services organisation operates 15 AI systems across business functions.

The organisation needs a structured way to identify AI use cases, collect system information, assess potential regulatory and operational risks, maintain evidence, assign accountability and monitor remediation actions.

This project will design and implement an AI Governance Readiness Workbench.

The workbench will combine:
- Structured AI system intake.
- AI-assisted information analysis.
- EU AI Act readiness screening.
- GDPR and privacy assessment routing.
- Security and third-party risk review.
- Human review and approval.
- Central AI system register.
- Risk and action tracking.
- Management reporting and audit evidence.

The AI component will support assessment preparation. It will not independently certify legal compliance or make final regulatory decisions.

## 2. Business Problem

Current-state scenario:

- AI system information is distributed across departments.
- Ownership and accountability are inconsistent.
- Regulatory assessment evidence is not centrally organised.
- Risk reviews depend on manual information gathering.
- Management lacks a consolidated view of AI governance readiness.
- Review actions and approvals are difficult to track consistently.

Business consequence:

The organisation has limited visibility of its AI portfolio and may struggle to demonstrate consistent governance, accountability and evidence management.

## 3. Project Objectives

1. Establish a central register for 15 fictional AI systems.
2. Standardise the collection of AI system information.
3. Provide AI-assisted preliminary regulatory screening.
4. Identify missing evidence and potential risk indicators.
5. Route assessments to appropriate human specialists.
6. Track controls, decisions, actions and approvals.
7. Provide management visibility through measurable KPIs.

## 4. Scope

### In scope

- AI inventory and system classification intake.
- Preliminary EU AI Act screening.
- GDPR and DPIA triage workflow.
- Security and supplier assessment routing.
- AI-assisted evidence summarisation.
- Human review and approval workflow.
- Risk register and remediation tracking.
- Monitoring and management dashboard.
- Test scenarios, UAT and operating procedures.

### Out of scope

- Legal advice or formal legal opinions.
- Automated legal compliance certification.
- Automatic final classification of AI systems.
- Production integration with a real financial institution.
- Processing real customer or employee personal data.
- Replacing Legal, DPO, CISO or other accountable specialists.

## 6. Delivery Approach

The project will use a hybrid delivery methodology.

### Stage 1 — Initiation
- Confirm business objectives.
- Identify stakeholders.
- Agree scope and governance.
- Establish delivery baseline.

### Stage 2 — Discovery
- Facilitate stakeholder workshops.
- Collect AI system information.
- Document current processes.
- Define functional and non-functional requirements.

### Stage 3 — Design
- Define solution architecture.
- Establish assessment framework.
- Define data and access controls.
- Design human approval workflow.

### Stage 4 — Build
- Configure intake forms.
- Configure AI-assisted assessment.
- Connect the system register.
- Configure workflow and reporting.

### Stage 5 — Validate
- Execute functional tests.
- Evaluate AI output quality.
- Conduct UAT.
- Resolve defects and control gaps.

### Stage 6 — Handover
- Complete documentation.
- Confirm ownership.
- Deliver training.
- Present final steering report.
- Capture lessons learned.

## 7. Proposed Success Measures

These are proposed acceptance targets, not achieved results.

| KPI                                     | Proposed target 

| AI system inventory completeness          100% of 15 systems 
| Required assessment fields completed     | At least 95% 
| Assessment records with traceable evidence | 100% 
| Critical scenarios correctly escalated in test set | 100% 
| AI output evaluation score | At least 90% on defined test criteria 
| Final risk decisions made by authorised humans | 100% 
| Critical defects open at handover | 0 
| UAT acceptance | Formal business owner sign-off 

AI output quality will be measured using a documented test set, not subjective impressions alone.

## 8. Governance and Decision Rights

- CIO: executive sponsorship and major escalations.
- AI Governance Lead: business decisions and policy ownership.
- Legal / Compliance: legal interpretation.
- DPO: privacy and DPIA decisions.
- CISO: security decisions.
- Project Manager: delivery coordination, reporting, risks, dependencies and change control.
- Technical specialists: solution configuration and technical validation.

The AI assistant cannot approve its own recommendations.

## 10. Estimation and Risk Method

Estimation methods:
- Bottom-up estimation for work packages.
- Three-point PERT estimation for uncertain activities.
- Dependency analysis and CPM for schedule planning.
- Contingency based on identified uncertainty and risk.

Risk management:
- Probability and impact assessment.
- Risk exposure and prioritisation.
- Mitigation owner and due date.
- Residual risk review.
- Immediate escalation of critical legal, privacy or security concerns.

## 11. AI CDR Framework

For this portfolio project, CDR means Compliance, Delivery and Risk. It is a project-specific operating framework, not a formal regulatory standard.

### C — Compliance
- Regulatory applicability screening.
- Evidence collection.
- Privacy and security review.
- Human accountability.
- Traceable decisions.

### D — Delivery
- Requirements and architecture.
- Implementation planning.
- Integration and testing.
- Change management.
- Operational handover.

### R — Risk
- AI output evaluation.
- Data quality and security risks.
- Human oversight.
- Bias and reliability testing.
- Monitoring and incident escalation.

## 13. Assumptions and Constraints

- All company and AI system data will be fictional.
- The demonstration will not be used for real legal decisions.
- Specialist reviewers will be represented in the workflow.
- Initial implementation will use free or low-cost tools where possible.
- The eight-week duration is a planning baseline.
- Budget and benefit figures will be estimated and labelled as illustrative until validated.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria

The project will be considered ready for portfolio demonstration when:

1. A user can submit an AI system intake record.
2. The application can generate a structured preliminary assessment.
3. Assessment outputs contain traceable source references.
4. Missing information is explicitly identified.
5. Potential risk indicators trigger human review.
6. The system records assessment history and decisions.
7. Test results and known limitations are documented.
8. Management can view system and risk status.
9. UAT evidence is available.
10. Operating and handover documentation is complete.
